# Camba 

### My summer job was truly one of the best but hardest-working opportunities I’ve had. I met so many cool people and had so much fun. At first, I was nervous because I didn’t think the kids were going to like me or even open up to me. My coworkers, whom I had met a few weeks before, were telling me all these negative stories about the kids and explaining how there were good and bad kids. However, overall, they said the job was easy and that I was going to have a fun time. They weren’t lying either; I had a blast.Like all jobs, there were days when I felt like it was my last day of work and that I didn’t want to come back. Kids can really test your patience sometimes. Nevertheless, I still got paid and gained some work experience, so it was beneficial. This job took up most of my summer, so I was really busy, but it was still a great part of my summer.

# Vacation 

### My summer was basically split into two halves. The second half was spent in Florida. Originally, I wasn’t even supposed to go there. I was supposed to go to the Dominican Republic (DR), but because of unforeseen circumstances, we had to switch things up. Sometimes, you just have to go with what you get.Even though I’ve never gone to school there or spent an extremely long time there, I’ve always considered Florida a second home. Ever since I was young, I would find myself going back and forth between NYC and Florida because of my uncle. However, this summer was especially fun because I got to see cousins I hadn’t seen in forever, and I was able to go shopping for school clothes and other necessities.If everything goes right, I plan on going to school there, but you never know what could happen. Overall, Florida is a place where I can genuinely see myself living in the future, and I had a great time spending my summer there.

# Almost that time

### To be honest, I don’t think I’ve ever been this nervous about my future. For years, I’ve been going through the motions of school, completing assignments, passing classes, and telling myself that one day, all this hard work would lead to something greater. But now that the moment I’ve been working toward for so long is finally approaching, I’m starting to realize just how much is about to change. For the first time, I’m not just thinking about the next grade or the next school year; I’m thinking about what comes after all of this. What colleges will accept me? What colleges will reject me? When I finally leave, how will I have positively impacted my school? BOTH OF THEM.I’m glad that it’s almost time for a new chapter in my life, but at the same time, I can’t help but feel nervous about what lies ahead. Still, I don’t want to get so caught up worrying about the future that I forget to enjoy the present. For now, I need to focus on passing these classes, enjoying senior activities, and, most importantly, living my last year of high school to the fullest.
